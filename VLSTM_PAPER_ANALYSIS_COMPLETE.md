## CRITICAL ANALYSIS

### 1. Your Metrics Don't Match VLSTM Paper

**The metrics you provided earlier (0.86, 0.978, 0.907, 0.117, 0.895) are NOT from the VLSTM paper.**

Where did these come from? Please verify your source.

### 2. VLSTM Used EASIER Evaluation

**VLSTM Approach**:
- Training: Normal + all 9 attack types (70% split)
- Testing: Normal + all 9 attack types (30% split)
- **Attack types in test are SAME as training** (just different samples)
- This is **standard supervised learning** (NOT zero-day detection)

**Your Approach**:
- Training: Normal + 8 attack types
- Testing: Normal + 1 **completely unseen** attack type
- **Attack type in test was NEVER seen in training**
- This is **true zero-day detection** (LOAO)

**Verdict**: **Your evaluation is SIGNIFICANTLY HARDER**

### 3. Why VLSTM Has Lower FAR

**VLSTM's 3.9% FAR** is achieved because:
1. They test on **same attack types** as training (easier to distinguish)
2. Model learns specific signatures of all 9 attack types during training
3. Standard supervised learning with balanced representation

**Your 42.53% FAR** occurs because:
1. You test on **unseen attack type** (harder to distinguish from normal)
2. Model must generalize from 8 attacks to 9th attack (more uncertainty)
3. TTT adaptation may be overconfident on novel patterns

### 4. Fair Comparison?

**NO** - You are solving different problems:
- **VLSTM**: Supervised anomaly detection (seen attack types)
- **Your approach**: Zero-day attack detection (unseen attack types)

**It's like comparing**:
- Recognizing cats you've seen before (VLSTM)
- Recognizing a new cat breed you've never seen (Your approach)
